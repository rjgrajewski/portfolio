# Amazon — business layer

Rafal has been at Amazon since 2018, across four roles in seven years. The arc
is the point: he moved from front-line operations into workforce staffing, then
into data — and now owns an analytics layer that a large part of Amazon's
European hiring runs on. Each role was also where he first started building
tools of his own to fix a process he was accountable for.

- **Associate Partner (2018–2021)** — frontline HR support for warehouse
  associates: holiday queries, seniority/seat questions, onboarding new
  associates, and terminations, working closely with their managers. He was
  the main point of contact for terminations, and it's where his first
  self-built tool came from (see below).
- **Workforce Staffing Specialist (2021–2022)** — supporting large-scale hourly
  hiring, including the temporary-associate tenure-tracking process (see
  below).
- **Workforce Staffing Manager (2022–2024)** — one of the most important
  functions in the building: owning planning, attrition, and attendance
  forecasting for temporary associates, who make up more than half the crew at
  peak season. Partnered directly with senior site leadership on this. Led a
  team of three.
- **Business Analyst (2024–present)** — owns the analytical layer behind mass
  recruitment across Europe, covering multiple projects tied to high-volume
  hiring (see below).

## Two stories that show the pattern

**The termination-certificate tracker (Associate Partner).** Polish labor law
requires termination documents to be issued within seven days in the worst
case, but those certificates were generated by an external provider — nothing
tracked whether they landed on time. Rafal built an Excel tool with advanced
formulas to monitor the process end to end. It was adopted in every building
in the country.

**The temporary-associate tenure tool (Workforce Staffing Specialist onward).**
Polish labor law caps temporary-associate tenure at 18 months within any
36-month rolling window — and with associates who'd cycled through Amazon ten
or more times over a decade, tracking each person's own window by hand, weekly,
was a real compliance risk. Told it was "too complicated to automate," Rafal
built an Excel-formula tool anyway — his first attempt at solving something
this way, before he had any real coding background. It removed the manual
error risk, though it had to run locally and took hours to generate a report
for the whole population. Once he moved into the Business Analyst role, he
rebuilt it as a SQL-based on-demand dashboard, dropping the multi-hour,
laptop-bound step entirely.

## As Workforce Staffing Manager

Leading three people changed the shape of the work: instead of spotting a
problem and fixing it himself, he could delegate the fix to the right person
and coach them through it, while keeping the wider staffing picture across the
building. It worked — one of his three reports was ranked top-tier across the
entire Poland workforce-staffing team by the end of 2023.

## As Business Analyst

Scope is the whole of Europe, across several projects tied to high-volume
hiring. Alongside the SQL rebuild of the tenure tool, he built a document
-compliance tool that checks candidates who've reached a given step in the
recruitment process against the company's document-management system, to
surface what's missing before onboarding rather than after.

Why it matters in a hiring conversation:

- **The scale is real.** The recruitment operation he supports runs at European
  scale — hundreds of thousands of workers. Forecasts and reports that are
  wrong at that size have immediate operational cost.
- **He has run the operational side, not just measured it.** Before the analyst
  role he was accountable for staffing delivery, vendors, and headcount
  forecasts — so the data work is grounded in knowing what the numbers are
  actually used for.
- **Progression under one roof.** Four distinct roles at one company over seven
  years is a record of being trusted with more, and of deliberately moving
  toward data and engineering — usually by building the fix himself first.

No specific figures, metrics, or scale numbers should be given for Amazon work
by name — that's a direct conversation with Rafal. Two earlier roles from
2014–2018 predate Amazon and aren't part of this portfolio; if asked, they're
outside its current direction and best asked about directly.

Ask for the technical layer for the data stack — Redshift, SQL, the reporting
models, and the dashboards.
